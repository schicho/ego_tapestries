Informed Consent

Study Title: BioFabric Study — Answering Questions About Networks with BioFabric Visualizations

Principal Investigator: Sara Di Bartolomeo, TU Wien

Purpose

You are invited to participate in a research study examining how different ways of drawing a network in the BioFabric visualization affect your ability to answer questions about the network. This study will take approximately [TODO: duration] minutes.

What You Will Do

You will view a series of network visualizations and answer questions about each one. First, a short tutorial explains how to read the visualization, followed by a short practice section so you can learn the interactions. During practice you will receive feedback on your answers, and you can only continue once your answer is correct. You have three attempts for each practice question: if none of them is correct, the study ends and you cannot continue. After the practice, a page tells you when the actual experiment starts. In the actual experiment you will not receive feedback. The actual experiment also contains one simple attention-check question. If you answer it incorrectly twice, the study ends and you cannot continue.

The experiment has three groups of questions, one for each type of question:

  1. Finding the nodes connected to a given node
  2. Finding the common neighbors of two given nodes
  3. Estimating the number of connections in a network

In the first two types of question, you will click nodes to select your answer and then submit it. In the third, you will type your estimate. After each group of questions, you will answer a short questionnaire about how demanding the questions were (the NASA Task Load Index, which uses sliders).

Participation

  • Participation is entirely voluntary. You may withdraw at any time without penalty, by closing the browser window.
  • There are no known risks beyond those of normal computer use.
  • Your responses will be recorded anonymously and used only for research purposes.
  • De-identified data may be shared in academic publications or archived in a public repository.

Data and Privacy

  • You are not asked for your name or any other personal information.
  • The data that is recorded are your answers, your response times, your interactions with the visualizations (such as which nodes you click), and your answers to the questionnaires.
  • The study software also records technical information about your browser session: your browser and operating system, your screen resolution, your browser language, and your IP address. The IP address is not part of the data that will be published or shared.
  • The data is stored securely on a server and is accessible only to the research team.
  • Data will be retained for a minimum of five years, in accordance with institutional policy.
