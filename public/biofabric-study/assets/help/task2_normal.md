## Help: select the common neighbors of two nodes

Find the two nodes highlighted in pink. A common neighbor is a node that is connected to both of them. The common neighbors are **below** the two nodes. Each of them has circles side by side on its horizontal line: one at the end of a connection (a vertical line) from the first node, and one at the end of a connection from the second node. Select every common neighbor.

### How to answer

Click a node, on its horizontal line or on its label, to select it. Click it again to remove the selection. The nodes highlighted in pink are given by the question: you cannot change them, and they are not part of your answer. Submit your answer once you have selected all the nodes.

## How to read BioFabric

BioFabric is a way of drawing a network so that its structure is easier to see, especially when the network contains many connections.

Each **node** is shown as a horizontal line running from left to right.

<img alt="Nodes are represented on horizontal lines" src="biofabric-study/assets/images/nodes-biofabric.png" width="50%" mx="auto" />

Each **edge** (also called a **connection**) is shown as a vertical line connecting the horizontal lines of the nodes it joins.

<img alt="Edges are represented as vertical connections" src="biofabric-study/assets/images/edges-biofabric.png" width="31.5%" mx="auto" />

The horizontal position of a node does not represent a location. Instead, nodes are arranged from top to bottom, and the vertical lines show which nodes are connected. A connection may cross other node lines; this does not mean that those nodes are connected. Follow the vertical edge line to identify its two endpoints.

<img alt="A connection between node 7 and node 5" src="biofabric-study/assets/images/full-biofabric.png" width="50%" mx="auto" />

The common neighbors of two nodes are the nodes that are connected to both of them. In the questions, the common neighbors are always **below** the two nodes. To find them, follow the connections down from each of the two nodes: a common neighbor has a circle at the end of a connection from the first node, and a circle at the end of a connection from the second node, on its own horizontal line.

<img alt="Nodes 3 and 5 have circles on connections from both node 7 and node 1, so they are common neighbors" src="biofabric-study/assets/images/common-neighbors-biofabric.png" width="50%" mx="auto" />

In the example, nodes 3 and 5 are connected to both node 7 and node 1, so they are common neighbors of node 7 and node 1.

To count the connections in a graph, count the vertical lines: each vertical line is one connection. In the questions, you only need to **estimate** the number of connections, so you do not need to count exactly.

In the examples that follow, use the horizontal lines to identify nodes and the vertical lines to trace edges between them.

### Selecting Nodes

The diagram is interactive: clicking a node, either on its horizontal line or on its label, highlights that node. Click the node again to remove the highlight.

In the questions, you will also use this to give your answers by selecting the nodes you want to answer with.
